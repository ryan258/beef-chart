# Beef cuts: names, relationships, and preparation

**English** · [Español (Argentina)](cuts-guide.es.md)

This is an educational reference for Argentine and U.S. beef. It is not a purchasing specification or a validated fabrication guide. Retail names vary by region, seller, bone removal, membrane, trim, and portioning, and the atlas geometry is schematic. Hover or keyboard focus highlights a broad relationship across the two maps; the decorative fill colors are not a shared anatomical key.

## Reading the illustrated atlas

The Argentine map has 20 cut controls. The U.S. map has nine region controls, with the front and hind shanks sharing one. Four specialty entries sit outside the body map, which brings the catalog to 24 cuts. Each dossier has a transparent halftone illustration. A steak portion, whole muscle, or bone-in preparation in the artwork helps you identify the cut; it is not proof that a price observation describes that exact form.

The angular cut-paper diagrams keep the broad order of the regions. Hatching marks lomo, entraña, and peceto as layered references. It does not mean those muscles are exposed on the same outer surface. U.S. regions carry permanent labels, and both maps show the full selected name on hover or focus. When a small region is hard to hit, use the full-name directory. Enter or Space opens a region's dossier.

Ribeye is drawn with a rounded central eye and cap. Sirloin has a broader, leaner, tapered silhouette. Tenderloin appears as a medallion and brisket as a slab, and the fore and hind shank references are drawn differently. Always confirm the actual seller's bone, trim, thickness, and portioning separately. Asset mappings and reference links are in [the artwork and diagram record](artwork-prompts.md).

The atlas works in English and in castellano porteño (Rioplatense Spanish). The header toggle translates all 24 dossiers, the diagrams, tables, and search feedback in place, and keeps both countries' reference names visible. Search covers both languages whichever one is displayed, ignores accents, and matches common aliases (*ojo de bife*, *tira de asado*, *achuras*).

## How to read a relationship

Three claims are easy to confuse and are not the same: an anatomical relationship, a similar cooking use, and an interchangeable purchase specification. Before you order, confirm species, muscle and boundaries, bone, membrane, trim, thickness, fresh or frozen state, and the standard and edition that apply. The price pairs in this atlas are contextual observations, not proof of identical products.

The core references are [Argentina's SENASA nomenclador, Resolution 1128/1999 and its original annex](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/resoluci%C3%B3n-1128-1999-60581/texto), [USDA IMPS purchasing specifications](https://www.ams.usda.gov/grades-standards/imps), and [USMEF's regional cut dictionary](https://usmefsudamerica.org/corte-de-res/). Using the original annex does not claim that later amendments or regional names have been reviewed. Uruguay's INAC manual can add regional context but is not an Argentine authority. Specialist acceptance of this atlas is still open.

## Practical preparation context

The cooking categories in the atlas are broad editorial suggestions. Cuts rich in connective tissue (neck, shank, chuck-region cuts, brisket) usually suit braising or other long cooking. Steak preparations vary with thickness, trim, equipment, and the result you want. Slice coarse-grained cuts across the grain. Membrane and fat treatment, and recipes, depend on the specific preparation, not on a translated cut name.

Avoid fixed-time promises. Neither a set number of minutes nor a preference for color or texture establishes a safe result. Check guidance for the product form and your jurisdiction, and use a food thermometer. [USDA FSIS's consumer temperature chart](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart) and [beef handling reference](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/meat-catfish/beef-farm-table) are U.S. consumer sources; an establishment's approved process, or another country, needs its own references. [Mechanically tenderized beef](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/meat/mechanically-tenderized-beef) needs handling that accounts for the product form.

The radar and the tenderness and marbling values are editorial heuristics. They are not measured sensory data or USDA grades, and preparation, animal, aging, and trim can change the result. Outbound recipe links are general web searches, not reviewed recipes or evidence of authenticity.

## Specialty entries (achuras and bone-in steak)

- **Mollejas:** sweetbreads. The primary label leaves the gland unspecified, and a generic retailer name does not prove thymus rather than another gland. Leave the gland unresolved unless the product says.
- **Riñón:** kidney. Keep raw and prepared products separate.
- **Lengua:** tongue. A whole raw tongue and a cooked or vinaigrette product are different observations.
- **Bife de costilla:** keep the bone-in qualification, and keep it separate from boneless strip and T-bone.

Achuras are part of many asado traditions, and menus and practices vary. These entries are not reviewed offal-safety instructions. The stored Carrefour organ observations (*mollejas*, *riñón*, *lengua*) have unverified unit scales, so their comparison prices are withheld and their native values are kept only as stored evidence until units and availability are verified. The Pecho, Cuadrada, and Bola de lomo dossiers use their own connected Argentine series when evidence exists; that connection does not establish an exact product match. Prices can be inactive catalog references or missing, and the atlas never substitutes another product's price to fill a gap.

## Reading price coverage and local quotes

The coverage table lists all 24 cuts separately for each country, including missing observations and prices withheld for unresolved unit scales. IPCVA is the primary automatic source for the AMBA monthly cuts it covers. Other sources keep their own geography, product, date, and availability qualifications. Retained retailer observations are dated references, not current offers. The fixed flow and value models and the editorial trait radar are illustrations, not measurements calculated from these prices.

You can enter a quote from a local butcher shop, or one a friend passed along, directly in the active manual file, following the [manual-price guide](manual-prices.md). Ten Argentine cuts are prefilled. Unfinished dates and prices are skipped, and valid completed quotes are labeled separately. Save and reload to see an edit. The shop's own wording, the city, and bone and trim detail can go in `item`; the six-field record needs no extra metadata. Ask for the observation date and an explicit per-kilogram price for the raw product, especially for the three organs. A manual quote is a separate observation. It does not verify the original Carrefour unit scale, gland identity, product equivalence, or current stock.
